What you just scrolled through

A stylised media campus — the long glass hull, the theatre drums, the roof gardens. Nothing here was downloaded. There is no model file and no image file: every mesh is written down in code, and every texture is painted into a <canvas> at runtime by a seeded generator, so the same 16 window tints and the same blades of grass come back on every load.

02

How it works

Four moving parts: a scroll timeline, a camera spline, three lighting rigs, and an exporter.

The timeline

Scroll is the build order

Parts are sorted by phase, then by height, and each one gets its own 10% slice of the scroll: it rises seven metres into position on a cubic ease-out. Scroll back up and the campus takes itself apart in the same order.

The camera

One spline, seven waypoints

A Catmull-Rom curve carries the camera from the south approach, around the back, and home again, while the look-at target climbs as the building grows. Drag at any time to take over — the drone orbit button hands it to a wandering radius-and-height loop instead.

The light

Morning, noon, dusk

Each preset paints its own sky gradient with clouds, then bakes that sky plus a sun sphere into a PMREM environment map — which is what the glass and bronze actually reflect. Haze drives the fog distances; at dusk the window emissives, the media screen and the street lamps come up.

The output

It leaves as a real model

Every mesh and material is named, so the campus exports straight from the browser as GLB or as OBJ + MTL — in metres, y-up, ready to open in Blender.

A likeness, not a survey. The proportions are drawn from the kind of media campus I walk into — a long curved hull over a green-glass podium, drums for the studios — but the geometry is hand-written, not measured. Rendering pauses whenever the stage scrolls out of view, and the whole thing falls back to a single still frame if you have reduced motion turned on.
