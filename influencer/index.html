The persona

Not an invented character. A likeness model trained on photographs of me, summoned by a trigger token, wearing my own voice. The niche is Singapore hawker food — the densest brand market on this island and the one I actually eat in.

02

The work

Reels, key stills, and short product spots. Shot at real stalls on real visits, then produced at a volume nobody holding a camera can match.

03

How it's built

Five stages, and the order matters — every one of them depends on the face holding still.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Thirty to forty photographs of me, all shot inside the same fortnight — same haircut, same weight — across a deliberate spread of angles, lighting and distance. Clothing and background change constantly, otherwise the model learns the shirt as part of my face.

  2. 02

    Train

    A Flux LoRA trained on that set, plus a voice clone from a few minutes of clean speech. Trained twice at different step counts and compared — overtraining welds the dataset's own poses into every output and the face stops being able to do anything new.

  3. 03

    Write

    Claude plans the week against the content pillars, writes the shot briefs, builds the image prompts, and drafts captions in English and Mandarin — natively, not machine-translated from the English.

  4. 04

    Generate and verify

    Every frame is scored against real photographs of me before it goes anywhere. Anything that drifts is discarded and regenerated automatically. Rejects are kept, not deleted — a drifting face is telling you something about the prompt.

  5. 05

    Publish

    Scheduled to Instagram, TikTok and YouTube through their official APIs, with each platform's AI-content label set programmatically rather than remembered. Metrics come back nightly and steer the next week's plan.

04

The identity gate

The one piece everything else rests on. Generative models drift: ask for the same person twice and you get cousins. An account dies the moment an audience notices.

Every generated image is embedded and scored against real photographs of me. Below threshold, it never reaches a queue, let alone a feed.

The threshold isn't guessed. Two genuine photographs of the same person in different light might only score 0.55 — demand 0.75 and you reject everything real; settle for 0.30 and you let strangers through. So the gate calibrates against the reference set itself: how alike are two real photographs of this person, and does the generated one clear that bar?

It also refuses to calibrate on a reference set that splits in two. A dataset holding photographs from two different haircuts passes every naive check — each half matches itself perfectly — while quietly producing a gate that means "resembles either version of him" and locks neither. That one surfaced as a failing test before it ever reached a photograph.

06

AI disclosure

The person is real. Most of the imagery is not. The photographs and videos published under this project are generated by an AI likeness model trained exclusively on photographs of me, with my consent.

No other real person is depicted, impersonated, or used as training data. The model is not based on any celebrity, public figure or third party, and it is never handed to a client.

All published content carries the AI-generated content label required by each platform — Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. Locations, meals and opinions reflect visits that actually happened: nothing is produced for a stall or a product I haven't experienced first-hand. A review of a meal I never ate is a lie about somebody's business, and no amount of automation makes that acceptable.
