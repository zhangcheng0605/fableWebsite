01

What you just scrolled through

The Gate of Heavenly Peace and the square it faces — the moat and its five marble bridges, the huabiao columns, the stone lions, the flagpole, the Monument to the People's Heroes, the Great Hall and the Museum down either flank, and the Mausoleum closing the far end. Nothing here was downloaded. There is no model file and no image file: every mesh is written down in code, and every texture — the glazed roof tiles, the studded doors, the window lattice, the bracket band under the eaves, the portrait and its two slogan boards — is painted into a <canvas> at runtime.

Ground

Beige earth to the horizon, a 1.6 km plate of paving over the square, Chang'an Avenue running east–west with its lane markings, and a sky painted as a three-stop gradient so the fog has something to fade into.

01

Gardens

Lawns and flower beds pop in along the approach — each bed a few hundred instanced spheres coloured by how far they sit from the centre, so the planting reads as concentric rings the way festival planting actually does.

02

Moat and five bridges

The Golden Water river, its two stone edges, and five arched bridges — each deck a slice of a 22 m cylinder, with balusters and hand rails following the curve step by step.

03

The gate wall

Five arched passages cut clean through 36 metres of wall: the wall is one extruded shape and the arches are holes in it, so they are real tunnels rather than dark rectangles painted on a box. Then the studded doors, the flanking walls with their tile coping and four wall pavilions, the portrait in its gilt frame, and the two slogan boards.

04

Terrace

The rostrum: a marble platform ringed by a balustrade of instanced posts, with fourteen flags along the parapet.

05

The hall below

Nine bays — twenty vermilion columns, lattice windows and doors front and back, the tie beam over them, and nine red lanterns hung between the columns.

06

The lower roof

The first of the two golden roofs, swept from a single profile function: the eaves widen and sag on a power curve while an extra term lifts only the diagonals, which is what gives the corners their upturn. Ridge beasts walk down every hip.

07

The upper storey

A shorter hall on the same plan, its own balustraded balcony, and the gilt emblem on the axis.

08

The upper roof

The same function again, wider and taller — the silhouette that puts the ridge 34 metres over the square.

09

Foreground

The pair of huabiao columns with their cloud discs and crouching beasts, the stone lions on their pedestals, the ornate lamp pair on the axis, and the crowd-control railings.

10

People

Nine hundred of them, in six instanced meshes — torso, head, hair, two legs, two arms — each with its own height, facing, jacket, hair and trousers. They are what make the rest read as architecture instead of a model on a table.

11

The square

Last, the far end: the flagpole on its double plinth, the Monument to the People's Heroes, the Great Hall and the National Museum facing each other across 700 metres, the Mausoleum, and then the trees and lamp standards that line the whole run.

02

How it works

Four moving parts: one profile function that makes every roof, a scroll timeline, a camera spline, and an exporter.

The roof

One function, six roofs

A hip roof is swept, not modelled: sixteen rings of seventy-two points, each ring wider and lower than the last on a power curve, with corner lift applied as |x·z|2.2 so it bites on the diagonals and nowhere else. The gate, both storeys and all four wall pavilions come out of the same call with different numbers.

The timeline

Scroll is the build order

Every piece owns a slice of the scroll and an entrance: drop slams it down out of the sky and bounces it once, pop bursts it into place with an overshoot, fade simply arrives. Scroll back up and the square takes itself apart in the same order.

The camera

Two splines, eight waypoints

One Catmull-Rom curve carries the eye, a second carries what it is looking at — from under the bridges, up past the rostrum, then away and up until the whole square is in frame. Drag at any time to swing around the current look-at point; scrolling takes the wheel back.

The crowd

900 people, 6 draw calls

Each person is six instances sharing one matrix — torso, head, hair, legs, arms — placed in four bands: the walkway, the foot of the wall, the open square, and around the Monument. Per-instance colour gives every one of them their own jacket, hair and trousers.

The output

It leaves as a real model

Every mesh and material is named, so the gate exports straight from the browser as GLB or as OBJ + MTL — in metres, y-up, ready to open in Blender.

A likeness, not a survey. The proportions come from photographs and from the shape everyone carries in their head — five arches, two golden roofs, the long red wall — but the geometry is hand-written, not measured, and the portrait over the central arch is painted into a canvas rather than photographed. Rendering pauses whenever the stage scrolls out of view, and the whole thing falls back to a single finished frame if you have reduced motion turned on.
